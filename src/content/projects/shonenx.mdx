## Overview & Motivation

If you've ever tried watching anime or reading manga on a computer or phone, you know the drill: your choices are either an abandoned Electron wrapper eating 2GB of idle RAM, or third-party streaming sites that open three popups for every play click.

I wanted something clean, snappy, and AMOLED-dark that actually treats your hardware with respect. An app that compiles to native code, runs smoothly on a budget phone or a Linux laptop without heating it up like a griddle, and doesn't inject telemetry or ads into your face.

ShonenX handles seamless watch and read tracking with AniList and MyAnimeList, lets you download episodes for commutes, and keeps media scrapers completely isolated via a modular extension system so the app stays fast and clean.

## Architecture & Decisions

The app is built around keeping heavy playback and network operations from stuttering the reactive UI layer.

<Point title="Solid Video Playback via media_kit">
Default video player packages in Flutter choke hard on complex SSA/ASS anime subtitles and custom audio tracks. Instead of fighting platform webviews, I used `media_kit`. It delegates video decoding, hardware acceleration, and subtitle rendering to `libmpv` under the hood so playback just works.
</Point>

<Point title="Decoupled Extension Architecture">
Hardcoding scrapers directly into an app is a recipe for maintenance nightmare. Built a sandboxed extension runtime so community sources can be updated independently without having to push a full client update every time a website changes a CSS class.
</Point>

<Point title="Offline-First Caching with Isar">
Used Isar NoSQL for instantaneous local queries on watch history, tracking queues, and downloaded chapters. If your Wi-Fi drops mid-transit, the app doesn't leave you staring at a dead white screen.
</Point>

## Engineering Challenges

<Challenge title="Memory Spikes on Rapid Video Scrubbing">
  <Problem>Aggressively seeking high-bitrate video streams on low-end Android devices would rapidly spike RAM usage and desync audio until Android's low-memory killer killed the process.</Problem>
  <Solution>Tuned mpv buffer limits, configured aggressive frame-dropping under memory pressure, and piped decoded video textures straight into Flutter's texture registry.</Solution>
</Challenge>

<Challenge title="Tracking Sync Conflicts">
  <Problem>Handling expired tokens and diverging watch progress across AniList and MyAnimeList simultaneously without accidentally overwriting newer progress.</Problem>
  <Solution>Wrote an optimistic offline mutation queue with idempotency keys and exponential backoff retry logic that reconciles tracking quietly when connection resumes.</Solution>
</Challenge>

## Stack & Rationale

<Stack>
  <StackItem name="Flutter & Dart" role="Cross-Platform Core">One codebase delivering genuine 60-120fps across mobile and desktop without spinning up a hidden browser instance.</StackItem>
  <StackItem name="media_kit" role="Video Playback">Wraps libmpv to handle hardware acceleration, tricky anime codecs, and styled subtitles without tears.</StackItem>
  <StackItem name="Riverpod" role="State Management">Compile-safe, testable state management that doesn't descend into spaghetti as features grow.</StackItem>
  <StackItem name="Isar DB" role="Persistence">Blazing fast embedded database with zero JSON serialization overhead.</StackItem>
</Stack>

## What I Learned

- Building for hundreds of active open-source users will teach you very quickly that edge cases you thought were impossible happen every single Tuesday.
- State management and buffer tuning matter far more than flashy UI tricks when building a video app for low-end mobile devices.
