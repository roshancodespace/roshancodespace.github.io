If you read my previous posts about `PHP` and `VB.NET`, you might be under the impression that I actually go to college to *learn* how to code. Let me clear that up right now: **I absolutely do not.**

College is just something I have to do. I skim the syllabus, nod along, and then wait until the week before mid-semesters to cram whatever legacy technology they want me to regurgitate. 

The real learning? That happens entirely on my own time. 

### Blessed With The Bug

I consider myself incredibly lucky. I didn't get into software engineering because someone told me it pays well or because it was a mandatory class. I got into it because I am genuinely, deeply obsessed with it. 

When you have that natural curiosity, you don't need a syllabus. You don't need a professor telling you what to do. You just build things. You break things. You fall down Wikipedia rabbit holes about memory management, you spend three hours debugging a `React` hook, or you spend an entire evening perfectly looping a pixel-art anime pet for your portfolio. 

### The "Schedule" (Or Lack Thereof)

If you ask me what my study schedule is, the most honest answer is: **I study everything, anytime.**

I don't have blocked-out "study hours" on a calendar. If I see a cool new framework drop on Twitter, I might drop everything and read the docs right then. If I get an idea for a cool UI interaction, I'll boot up my editor and just start typing. 

But if I had to pin down my absolute peak productivity hours? It's always at night.

### The Night Shift

There is something inherently magical about coding at 2:00 AM. 

The world is completely quiet. There are no Slack notifications going off, no family members walking in, and no guilt about "wasting the day." It’s just you, your monitor, a dark theme editor (shoutout to `poimandres`), and a terminal. 

Most of my biggest breakthroughs and best projects have happened in the dead of night. Your brain enters this weird flow state where time stops existing. You start writing a quick script at midnight, and suddenly you look up, the sun is rising, and you've accidentally built an entire full-stack `Next.js` application.

### The Real Curriculum

While college is busy teaching procedural `PHP` and `VB.NET` modules, my personal night-shift curriculum looks completely different. It's chaotic, but it's practical.

One week I might be deep into `TypeScript` generics. The next, I’m figuring out how `Astro` islands work. Then I might pivot entirely and learn how to optimize `CSS` animations so they run smoothly on low-end devices. 

It’s completely unstructured, but because I’m following my own curiosity, it sticks. I learn faster, I retain more, and most importantly, I actually enjoy the process.

### Conclusion

So yeah, if you're wondering how I manage to balance college with actually learning modern software engineering... I don't. College is just a side quest. The main storyline happens late at night, fueled by caffeine, curiosity, and an unhealthy amount of open browser tabs!
